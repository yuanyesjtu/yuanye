01 / PROFILE

About me

I am Ye Yuan, a Lecturer at the Institute of Machine Intelligence, University of Shanghai for Science and Technology (USST). My research focuses on embodied affective interaction, robot vision and brain-inspired computing. I bring together multimodal machine learning, robotic control and computational neuroscience to investigate humanoid robots capable of natural expression and interaction.

My current work spans speech-driven emotional facial animation, facial kinematics and expression imitation, multimodal emotion recognition in conversation, and visual perception and localization in dynamic environments. In parallel, I study neural connectivity inference from calcium activity, neuronal network dynamics and models of locomotion behavior.

I received my B.Eng. in Automation from Chongqing University in 2015 and my Ph.D. in Control Science and Engineering from Shanghai Jiao Tong University in 2020. I was a visiting researcher at the Queensland Brain Institute, Australia, and subsequently a postdoctoral researcher at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. I joined USST in 2022, lead an NSFC Young Scientists Fund project, and was selected for USST’s Sixue Program.

02 / RESEARCH

Research

01

Embodied affective interaction

Integrating speech, vision and language for emotion understanding, speech-driven facial animation and multimodal foundation-model-supported interaction, connecting perception with physical expression.

02

Robot vision & bio-inspired control

Studying facial landmark forecasting, expression-to-actuator mapping, imitation learning and visual SLAM to connect data-driven models with motion on physical robots.

03

Brain-inspired computing & neuroscience

Using graph neural networks, structural plasticity and dynamical modeling to investigate neural connectivity, signal transmission and locomotion, and the computational principles of biological intelligence.

From learning algorithms to embodied interaction

Our team uses biomimetic expressive robots to study facial motion control, multimodal perception and generative interaction, with exploratory applications in science–art performances, education and public guidance. Related robots have appeared in City of Giants, Guangfulin Kunqu performances and World Artificial Intelligence Conference demonstrations.
