About me

I am Ye Yuan, a Lecturer at the Institute of Machine Intelligence, University of Shanghai for Science and Technology (USST). My research focuses on embodied affective interaction in robotics, brain-inspired computing and computational neuroscience. I received my B.Eng. in Automation from Chongqing University in 2015 and my Ph.D. in Control Science and Engineering from Shanghai Jiao Tong University in 2020. Following a research visit to the Queensland Brain Institute, Australia, and postdoctoral research at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, I joined USST in 2022. I study emotion understanding and facial control in robots, alongside neural connectivity inference and locomotion modeling. I have served as principal investigator of an NSFC Young Scientists Fund project and was selected for USST’s Sixue Program.

Research

Embodied affective interaction in robotics

Investigating how robots can understand human emotions, generate facial expressions consistent with speech and interaction context, and translate those expressions into natural, continuous physical motion. Topics include multimodal emotion recognition, speech-driven facial animation, facial landmark forecasting, and expression-to-actuator mapping and control.

Brain-inspired computing and computational neuroscience

Investigating how neural connectivity shapes signal transmission and locomotion, and how network structure and dynamics can be inferred from observations. This work combines graph learning, structural plasticity and neuromechanical modeling to study calcium activity, network evolution and C. elegans locomotion.

Robotic research platform

Our team uses expressive humanoid robots to study the path from visual perception and facial representation to actuator control, with experiments in expression imitation and multimodal interaction. Related methods are described in our work on sign-language recognition with facial mirroring and two-stage facial kinematic control.
